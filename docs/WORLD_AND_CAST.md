# World and Cast: Vaudrey House narrative checkpoint

Status: accepted working narrative foundation. This document preserves the agreed setting and character architecture so it does not depend on chat history. Revise deliberately; do not casually replace these elements while implementing presentation or dialogue.

The engine remains authoritative for rules. This document is authoritative for the current fictional direction.

## Core design principle

The game is about the circulation, suppression, and reconstruction of knowledge.

The mystery changes from game to game. The biographies do not.

There are two distinct kinds of guilt:

- Historical guilt is fixed. It concerns the Calder Works disaster and what the inhabitants of Vaudrey House did before and after it.
- Procedural guilt is generated. It is the current game's solution: who intercepted Sir Clement Vaudrey's sealed deposition, by what stratagem, and where it was concealed.

Solving procedural guilt does not determine who is morally worst. It determines who took the document tonight.

The engine's suspect / method / location ontology remains unchanged. Presentation may call methods "stratagems."

## Working identity and tone

Working world/title phrase: **Clockwork Testimony**.

Setting: Vaudrey House, England, late 1890s.

Tone: Wilkie Collins-inflected sensation mystery with restrained steampunk. Avoid costume-pageant "goggles and gears" treatment. Machinery should exist inside a recognizable late-Victorian domestic, legal, industrial, and social world.

Sir Clement Vaudrey made his fortune in telegraphy, pneumatic dispatch, precision instruments, signalling systems, and private communications. Vaudrey House incorporates an internal pneumatic dispatch network, electrical annunciators, mechanical registers and clocks, a telegraph room, workshops, and a small power plant.

The house should embody the theme: information literally moves through its walls.

## Present-tense mystery

After Sir Clement's death, trustees prepare to open a sealed deposition concerning his final years and the Calder Works disaster.

The deposition is sent through Vaudrey House's private dispatch system from the Archive toward the room where it is to be opened.

The receiving canister arrives.

The deposition does not.

The player must determine:

1. Who intercepted it.
2. By what stratagem.
3. Where it was concealed.

The theft need not imply an intention to destroy the deposition. A culprit may want to suppress it, preserve it, read it first, copy it, bargain with it, or prevent another person from reaching it first.

That multiplicity is required for replayability: every suspect must have plausible motives both to control the deposition and to keep it in existence.

## The fixed historical event: Calder Works

Seventeen years before the game, Vaudrey & Pike demonstrated an experimental automatic signalling system at Calder Works.

The system had an intermittent sequencing fault. Under certain circumstances, two signals could arrive out of order and briefly indicate that a restricted section was clear when it was not.

Gideon Pike recommended postponing the demonstration.

Sir Clement overruled him because cancellation threatened an important contract.

During the demonstration a supervisor received a false clear indication and restarted machinery while workers remained inside a restricted section. Four people died.

The junior operator, Samuel March, was blamed.

The official account was false.

Clement did not deliberately cause the deaths. His defining wrongdoing occurred after the accident: he recognized that the truth threatened his company and deliberately rearranged the evidence so that responsibility settled upon a man he knew had not caused the disaster.

The signalling system was redesigned. The company survived. Patents derived from the work became the foundation of the Vaudrey fortune.

The game should preserve this distinction:

Clement accepted a known risk before Calder.

After Calder, he consciously converted negligence into deception.

## The sealed deposition

The deposition is fixed history. It does not identify the current game's thief. Instead, it contains revelations capable of damaging or vindicating every suspect.

### 1. Samuel March was innocent

Samuel March followed the emergency protocol correctly and did not cause the fatal restart.

The control record used against him was altered after the accident.

Clement admits that he knew March was innocent before the public inquiry ended and nevertheless allowed the accusation to stand.

Samuel later died by suicide.

This is the moral center of the Calder history and the central fact sought by his daughter, Sabine.

### 2. Clement created the conditions for the disaster

The intermittent signalling fault was known before the demonstration.

Gideon Pike recommended postponement.

Clement overruled him.

A supervisor elsewhere in the works acted upon the false clear indication. Clement did not order the fatal restart, but his decision to proceed knowingly exposed workers to the risk that produced it.

### 3. Gideon Pike falsified a different technical record

Pike's warning about the fatal signalling fault was genuine.

After Calder, however, Pike altered a laboratory record concerning a separate defect in the system because he feared investigators would declare the entire design unsound.

That defect did not cause the deaths.

Pike's falsification nevertheless gave Clement leverage over him. Their later celebrated partnership became partly an arrangement of mutual captivity.

The deposition also states that the essential invention was Pike's. Clement financed, patented, commercialized, and eventually received public credit for work that was fundamentally Gideon's.

Thus the same document can restore Pike's authorship and expose his falsification.

### 4. The witness testimony was manufactured

The inquiry contained more than one lie.

Clement directed witnesses toward firmer recollections than they actually possessed. Statements were rewritten. Chronology was adjusted. At least one witness received money afterward.

Miriam Saye prepared revised statements at Clement's direction.

She secretly retained carbon copies of originals and earlier drafts.

Clement eventually discovered that she had preserved them and, by then, deliberately allowed her to keep them. Miriam therefore became both participant in the deception and custodian of evidence capable of undoing it.

### 5. The Calder settlements were fraudulent

Families of the dead received compensation in exchange for surrendering further claims.

Tobias Rook's father administered the settlements.

One widow refused.

Her signature nevertheless appears on the final agreement.

Clement admits the signature was forged.

Certain patent rights and later corporate assets passed through the same legal structure. The problem therefore survives into the present estate: portions of Vaudrey property may rest upon defective title.

Tobias discovered the questionable signature years later and deliberately avoided investigating it.

His fixed wrongdoing is preservation through inaction, not the original forgery.

### 6. Clement attempted confession before

Years before his death, Clement began writing an account of Calder.

Lady Honora Vaudrey discovered part of it and destroyed several pages. At the time she believed Clement was in one of his recurrent collapses and was about to destroy his family over events that could no longer be repaired.

Clement knew what she had done and never confronted her.

During his final illness, he also began telling Dr. Elias Fenwick about Calder.

Fenwick believed Clement did not know that one of the dead workers was Fenwick's elder brother.

Clement did know.

He continued employing Fenwick partly because he wanted someone personally connected to the dead to hear the confession.

During those final attempts Fenwick increased Clement's morphia. Fenwick believed the dose medically justified, but he knew it might prevent Clement from completing what he was trying to say.

The deposition refuses to decide Fenwick's motive for him: treatment and silencing remain morally entangled.

## Governing sentence

The mystery is not what happened at Calder.

Everyone at Vaudrey House has been living inside what happened at Calder for seventeen years.

The present mystery is what Sir Clement finally decided to say about it, and who took control of that testimony before the others could hear it.

## Suspects

### Lady Honora Vaudrey

Public position: Clement's widow and present mistress of Vaudrey House.

Core disposition: disciplined, socially formidable, protective of continuity and reputation.

Fixed history:
- She destroyed pages from Clement's earlier attempted confession.
- She knows enough about the Calder aftermath to understand that exposure can damage living people as well as the dead.
- She has spent years distinguishing, in her own mind, between preserving a family and perpetuating a lie.

Reasons to control the deposition:
- It can destroy the Vaudrey name.
- It can destabilize the estate.
- It may clear people harmed by Clement.
- It may reveal that Clement understood her destruction of his earlier confession.

Do not write Honora as a simple defender of respectability. She can genuinely believe that truth delivered without regard for consequence is another form of violence.

### Gideon Pike

Public position: Clement's chief engineer and celebrated collaborator.

Core disposition: technically exacting, proud, emotionally economical, unable to forget intellectual theft.

Fixed history:
- He warned Clement not to proceed with the Calder demonstration.
- He altered a separate laboratory record after the disaster.
- Much of the invention attributed to Clement was Gideon's.
- Clement used Gideon's falsification as leverage after Calder.

Reasons to control the deposition:
- It can restore his authorship.
- It can expose his evidence tampering.
- It can destroy the public story of the Vaudrey-Pike partnership.
- It can reveal how long he accepted the arrangement.

Do not turn Gideon into a pure wronged genius. He was exploited, and he also compromised himself.

### Miriam Saye

Public position: Clement's former private secretary.

Core disposition: precise, observant, procedurally powerful, accustomed to being underestimated.

Fixed history:
- She prepared revised Calder witness statements.
- She knew the revisions differed from earlier testimony.
- She secretly kept carbon copies.
- Clement later learned she had preserved them and did not force their destruction.

Reasons to control the deposition:
- It can establish that she acted under Clement's direction.
- It can expose that she knowingly helped manufacture the record.
- It can validate the evidence she has preserved for seventeen years.
- It may finally force her to explain why she possessed exculpatory material and remained silent.

Miriam's power is documentary memory: she remembers how decisions became paperwork.

### Dr. Elias Fenwick

Public position: physician who treated Clement during his final decline.

Core disposition: clinically controlled, morally severe, capable of confusing restraint with detachment.

Fixed history:
- His elder brother was one of the four people killed at Calder.
- Elias entered the Vaudrey household years later believing Clement did not know the connection.
- Clement did know.
- Clement attempted confession during his final illness.
- Elias increased Clement's morphia, medically justifiably but with knowledge that it might silence him.

Reasons to control the deposition:
- It may establish the truth about his brother's death.
- It may expose the ambiguity of his treatment of Clement.
- It may prove that the personal secret around which he organized years of his life was never secret from Clement.
- It may force him to confront whether he wanted a confession, revenge, or both.

Do not resolve the morphia question into secret murder. The uncertainty is stronger.

### Sabine March

Public position: writer preparing an authorized life of Sir Clement Vaudrey.

Core disposition: intellectually aggressive, controlled in public, relentless about documentary contradiction.

Fixed history:
- "March" is not merely a professional connection: Samuel March, the operator blamed for Calder, was her father.
- Samuel later died by suicide.
- Sabine accepted the Vaudrey commission specifically to obtain access to private records.
- She has built much of her adult life around the possibility that the official Calder story is false.

Reasons to control the deposition:
- It can clear her father.
- It can prove Clement knowingly sacrificed him.
- It may contradict the history Sabine has constructed and therefore threaten the identity she has built around seeking vindication.
- She may want publication; she may also need to know its contents before anyone can suppress, edit, litigate, or contextualize them.

Sabine has the most obvious revenge motive, so dialogue and staging must take care not to make her the default culprit.

### Tobias Rook

Public position: family solicitor, executor, and trustee.

Core disposition: controlled, legalistic, conservative in the literal sense: he preserves structures because dismantling them creates consequences he cannot predict.

Fixed history:
- His father handled the Calder settlements.
- Tobias later discovered evidence that one settlement signature was forged.
- He recognized that present estate assets may depend upon legally defective transactions.
- He deliberately avoided a full investigation.

Reasons to control the deposition:
- It may expose his father's fraud.
- It may demonstrate Tobias's own knowing inaction.
- It may destabilize ownership of important estate assets.
- It could also finally give him grounds to unwind legal arrangements he has privately come to regard as indefensible.

Do not make Rook merely venal. His moral danger is the belief that continuity itself can justify concealment.

## Provisional investigators

Keep three investigators for the current design.

Current names:
- Rowan
- Lena
- Otis

They are investigators, not suspect cards.

Their identities should eventually embody different epistemic habits rather than merely different voices:

- Rowan: provenance. Who knew what, when, and from what source?
- Lena: contradiction. Which claims cannot all be true?
- Otis: mechanism. What sequence, access, and physical possibility can actually produce the event?

These are present character directions, not yet implemented bot policies. Do not claim the baseline bot already reasons differently by investigator.

Three investigators fit the planned full content particularly well: 6 suspects + 6 stratagems + 9 locations = 21 cards; after the 3-card solution is removed, 18 cards remain, allowing six cards per investigator.

## Stratagems

Current full-game working set:

1. Copied key
2. Forged summons
3. Switched dispatch canister
4. Reversed pneumatic valve
5. Disabled alarm circuit
6. Assumed messenger identity

These are not weapons. They are mechanisms for manipulating access, authority, machinery, records, and trust.

Internal engine terminology remains "method."

## Locations

Current full-game working set:

1. Drawing Room
2. Gallery
3. Library
4. Archive
5. Telegraph Room
6. Workshop
7. Engine Room
8. Steward's Office
9. Winter Garden

The location graph should matter. Do not make every room adjacent to every other room.

Working topology:

- Drawing Room <-> Gallery
- Gallery <-> Library
- Library <-> Archive
- Archive <-> Telegraph Room
- Telegraph Room <-> Workshop
- Workshop <-> Engine Room
- Engine Room <-> Steward's Office
- Steward's Office <-> Drawing Room
- Gallery <-> Winter Garden
- Winter Garden <-> Workshop

This produces a principal circuit plus a secondary cross-route through the Winter Garden.

The architecture also carries thematic meaning:
- Drawing Room and Gallery: public display and social performance.
- Library and Archive: institutional memory.
- Telegraph Room and Workshop: communication and invention.
- Engine Room and Steward's Office: concealed infrastructure and administration.
- Winter Garden: an intermediary space between domestic order and engineered environment.

## Tutorial versus full game

The existing 3 x 3 x 3 guided practice should remain small unless user testing shows a reason to change it.

Its current provisional content exists to teach deduction. Do not expand the tutorial merely to match the full game's scale.

The full Play a game content should become the 6-suspect, 6-stratagem, 9-location Vaudrey House world.

The tutorial's "missing dispatch" is thematically compatible with the full game and may eventually be reframed as a miniature training case within the same world, but its existing teaching logic should not be broken merely for narrative consistency.

## Relationship architecture

The six suspects should not behave as isolated biographies. Their dialogue should emerge from an asymmetric network of debts, suspicions, withheld knowledge, old alliances, and private recognitions.

The following dyads are current working relationships.

### Honora and Gideon

Public surface: long-practiced civility between the widow and her husband's surviving partner.

Private structure:
- Honora knows Clement appropriated more of Gideon's work than the public record admits.
- Gideon knows Honora destroyed pages from Clement's earlier confession.
- Neither knows exactly how much documentary proof the other possesses.

Dynamic: mutual deterrence. Each regards the other as compromised but not contemptible.

Dialogue pressure: they can speak politely while continually testing whether the other is about to defect from the old compact.

### Honora and Miriam

Public surface: former mistress and former secretary, with the class distinction still audible.

Private structure:
- Miriam knows Honora destroyed part of Clement's earlier confession.
- Honora strongly suspects Miriam retained Calder papers, but does not know the extent of the carbon-copy archive.
- Miriam has repeatedly allowed Honora to underestimate her documentary reach.

Dynamic: intimacy distorted by hierarchy. They know one another's habits better than either admits.

Dialogue pressure: Honora can command almost everyone in the house except Miriam's memory.

### Honora and Fenwick

Public surface: widow and physician discussing Clement's final illness.

Private structure:
- Honora suspects Fenwick sometimes used sedation not merely for pain but to prevent Clement's agitation and confession.
- Fenwick remembers occasions when Honora herself asked that Clement be "kept quiet" for the sake of his health and the household.
- Neither can prove malign intent in the other.

Dynamic: mirrored accusation. Each sees in the other a possible version of the compromise they fear in themselves.

Dialogue pressure: medical language and domestic language become competing euphemisms for control.

### Honora and Sabine

Public surface: patron and authorized biographer.

Private structure:
- Honora approved Sabine's access because she believed an authorized biography could stabilize Clement's posthumous reputation.
- Sabine entered the house under a purpose Honora did not understand.
- Honora has begun to notice that Sabine asks questions with an intimacy no ordinary biographer should possess, but has not yet established why.

Dynamic: patronage becoming investigation in both directions.

Dialogue pressure: every interview can reverse who is examining whom.

### Honora and Rook

Public surface: widow and trustee jointly preserving the estate.

Private structure:
- Rook knows how badly the deposition could destabilize Honora's material position.
- Honora knows Rook's father managed the Calder settlements and suspects Tobias has found something in those files.
- They depend on one another institutionally while distrusting one another morally.

Dynamic: alliance without confidence.

Dialogue pressure: both naturally speak in the language of consequences rather than truth.

### Gideon and Miriam

Public surface: two long-serving members of Clement's old professional household.

Private structure:
- After Calder, Gideon asked Miriam to preserve anything that might prove he had warned Clement.
- Miriam did more than he asked: she preserved material capable of exposing the entire manufactured inquiry.
- Gideon later retreated from confrontation with Clement. Miriam never forgave that retreat.
- Gideon does not know everything Miriam kept.

Dynamic: failed alliance. They once came close to acting together and then chose different forms of survival.

Dialogue pressure: Miriam can remind Gideon that possessing the truth and using it are different acts.

### Gideon and Fenwick

Public surface: engineer and physician, each treating the other's expertise with guarded skepticism.

Private structure:
- Fenwick has spent years trying to reconstruct the physical sequence of the Calder accident.
- Gideon has given him technically true but incomplete answers because full explanation would expose Gideon's altered laboratory record.
- Gideon suspects Fenwick's interest in Calder is personal, but does not know the precise family connection.

Dynamic: mechanism versus motive.

Dialogue pressure: Fenwick wants the causal chain; Gideon knows that causality and culpability are not identical.

### Gideon and Sabine

Public surface: biographer interviewing the surviving engineer.

Private structure:
- Gideon has gradually realized that Sabine knows details available only to the March family or to someone who has seen private Calder material.
- Sabine suspects Gideon warned Clement before the demonstration but cannot yet prove it.
- Neither has openly acknowledged what they have inferred about the other.

Dynamic: mutual recognition without confession.

Dialogue pressure: their interviews can become highly technical duels in which both understand the unstated question.

### Gideon and Rook

Public surface: longstanding disputes over patents, royalties, and ownership.

Private structure:
- Rook possesses instruments showing that Gideon transferred rights to Clement under circumstances Gideon considers coercive.
- Gideon knows the Rook firm structured those transfers and believes Tobias has hidden behind his father's actions.
- Tobias privately believes Gideon has a legitimate authorship claim but fears reopening it would unravel broader estate rights.

Dynamic: explicit hostility grounded in documents.

Dialogue pressure: each knows exactly which piece of paper the other does not want examined.

### Miriam and Fenwick

Public surface: respectful familiarity from Clement's final years.

Private structure:
- Miriam recognized years ago that Fenwick's interest in Calder exceeded professional curiosity.
- She eventually identified his family connection but never disclosed that she knew.
- Fenwick has long suspected Miriam knows more about the original witness statements than she admits.

Dynamic: two keepers of different forms of evidence: documentary and bodily.

Dialogue pressure: each can tell when the other is withholding, but neither knows the full inventory.

### Miriam and Sabine

Public surface: secretary turned archival guide and authorized biographer.

Private structure:
- Miriam recognized Sabine's identity earlier than Sabine realizes.
- Miriam has quietly enabled Sabine to encounter certain records without directly handing over the carbon copies.
- Sabine believes some discoveries were accidents or the result of her own persistence.

Dynamic: covert mentorship complicated by guilt.

Dialogue pressure: if Sabine learns how long Miriam possessed exculpatory evidence, gratitude can become fury instantly.

### Miriam and Rook

Public surface: old administrative colleagues with impeccable manners.

Private structure:
- Rook suspects Miriam retained unauthorized Calder copies.
- Miriam knows Tobias discovered the questionable settlement signature and chose not to investigate.
- Both understand records well enough to recognize deliberate absence.

Dynamic: archival cold war.

Dialogue pressure: they can threaten one another by asking innocent questions about missing files.

### Fenwick and Sabine

Public surface: physician and biographer with a shared interest in Calder's dead.

Private structure:
- Fenwick recognizes the emotional pattern in Sabine's investigation before he knows or admits exactly who she is.
- Sabine has discovered that Fenwick lost a close relative at Calder but not the full history of his entry into Clement's household.
- Each is suspicious of the other's claim to dispassionate inquiry.

Dynamic: competing forms of bereavement transformed into investigation.

Dialogue pressure: each can accuse the other of using the dead to justify present choices.

### Fenwick and Rook

Public surface: physician and solicitor with little natural affinity.

Private structure:
- Rook discovered Fenwick's family connection through old legal records.
- He has never exposed it, partly because it would raise questions about Clement's decision to retain Fenwick.
- Fenwick knows enough about the Calder compensation process to suspect the Rook firm's role but not the forged signature.

Dynamic: leverage held in reserve.

Dialogue pressure: Rook knows a secret Fenwick thinks is still concealed; Fenwick senses the asymmetry without knowing its exact source.

### Sabine and Rook

Public surface: researcher requesting access from a trustee who prefers controlled disclosure.

Private structure:
- Sabine has found inconsistencies in the settlement archive and believes Rook knows more than he admits.
- Rook has discovered that Sabine accessed material beyond the scope originally granted to her.
- Neither wants to force an open confrontation because each still needs access to something controlled by the other.

Dynamic: adversaries compelled to cooperate.

Dialogue pressure: every request for a document is also a negotiation over what each already knows.

## Network-level design rules

1. No suspect is the canonical villain.
2. Every suspect has committed, enabled, concealed, or prolonged something morally consequential.
3. Every suspect also possesses at least one legitimate claim against Clement or the system around him.
4. Every suspect must have a plausible reason to preserve the deposition as well as a plausible reason to suppress or control it.
5. Dialogue must not leak the generated culprit through fixed characterization.
6. Character suspicion should arise from conflicting obligations and incomplete knowledge, not villain mannerisms.
7. At least some relationships should remain genuinely affectionate or respectful despite conflict. The house should not feel populated by six people who simply hate one another.
8. Asymmetric knowledge is preferred over symmetrical secret-sharing because it produces more useful conversational tension.
9. Fixed historical revelations may emerge gradually through optional dialogue, transcripts, documents, or later narrative systems, but the deduction engine must not require the player to know them in order to solve the procedural suspect / stratagem / location case.
10. Do not make historical guilt mathematically solvable through the card system. It belongs to narrative interpretation.

## Next narrative work

The next design layer is to turn this relationship architecture into playable characterization without prematurely generating large dialogue libraries.

Recommended sequence:

1. Define each suspect's speaking style, social tactics, verbal evasions, and subjects they refuse to discuss.
2. Define which fixed facts the player can learn during an ordinary game and by what channels.
3. Decide whether the generated culprit receives only neutral procedural lines or a small culprit-safe variation layer that cannot reveal guilt mechanically.
4. Define investigator personalities more fully and decide later whether they justify distinct bot policies.
5. Convert the full-game content into a separate Vaudrey House content pack while preserving the small guided tutorial.
6. Only after those contracts are stable, design voice casting, generated dialogue, art direction, and state-driven music.
