## 1. Retained commitments

Retain the original-world, finite-category deduction game; fixed biographies and history with a variable present-case culprit; control of evidence as the central dramatic conflict; a contained late-Victorian industrial household; and text-first, manually paced play that does not require visual interpretation or custom audio.

Retain six suspects and nine locations as the working full-game scale. Propose six concealment objects instead of six overlapping stratagems. This is a deliberate narrative-category change for review, not a claim that the user already requested that change. It can occupy the engine's existing method slot, but requires appropriate labels and content integration. It is not an additional fourth category.

Keep the current three-investigator teaching prototype intact. New full-game characters are not replacements for the tutorial labels until a separately tested content pack exists.

Working setting name: Bellwether House. Do not lock a commercial title yet.

## 2. The premise

The woman who ordered the diversion of the flood is hosting dinner.

Ada Harrow once directed the private signal exchange serving an English estuary town. During a catastrophic tide twelve years ago, she ordered the South Cut opened to protect the main pumping works. Water was diverted toward the inhabited lower streets. People died there; the pumping works survived.

The public report said those streets had been warned before the gate was opened. Their residents disputed that account from the beginning.

A recently recovered receiver roll records the gate-opening acknowledgement before the lower-street warning transmission. It does not explain every human decision. It does contradict the sequence in the approved report.

Bellwether House, Ada's home and the former signal exchange, is being prepared for an archive handover. Six people meet to settle how the recovered record will be held and presented. By the end of their private consultation, the original has disappeared.

One of those six removed it, concealed it inside one of six portable objects, and left that object in one of nine rooms.

The theft is the variable case. The flood and the relationships it altered are fixed history.

The original's disappearance does not erase every copy or every witness. It interrupts independent examination of the physical record and puts control of its first public presentation into dispute.

## 3. The house and period

Story time: late autumn 1898. The flood occurred in late autumn 1886, twelve years earlier. Ages below are approximate at the story date and consistent with that interval.

The town is fictional. Period-inspired machinery and institutional practices are authored for this setting; exact historical authenticity has not been certified. Do not present a fictional recording system, museum agreement or procedure as verified Victorian practice.

Bellwether House was designed to let a household and a working signal exchange occupy the same building. Domestic rooms join rooms for dispatch, charts, instruments, repairs and records. The old apparatus was once useful infrastructure, not decoration installed to suggest steampunk.

Much of it is now idle. A clockwork annunciator can still ring in an empty office. A pneumatic carrier arrives with an ordinary packing list rather than a prophecy. The same house contains dinner arrangements, obsolete equipment, employees' belongings and the apparatus through which an consequential order once passed.

The social scale is intimate: not a mansion populated by a cast whose only occupation is having secrets, but a former workplace whose proprietor never entirely stopped running it.

The technology must be understandable in ordinary language. It records transmission and acknowledgement; it does not detect guilt, read minds or reveal unspoken motives.

## 4. Fixed history and the record's limits

### Authored historical sequence

H01. A severe tide threatened the pumping works and surrounding districts. The South Cut could be opened to divert water away from the works, at the expense of the lower streets.

H02. Ada ordered the diversion without waiting for confirmation that the lower streets had been warned and cleared. Her purpose was to save the pumping works. She understood that people in the diversion area were at risk. The fact that she did not desire their deaths does not remove her causal responsibility.

H03. The fictional exchange's receiver roll records an acknowledgement from the gate apparatus before it records the warning transmission to the lower-street bell circuit. The relevant ordering comes from one continuous recording sequence, not an unexplained comparison of separately synchronized clocks.

H04. Eight residents died in the flooding of the lower streets. Do not infer from the roll alone the exact time water reached each building, which individuals heard a bell, or the effect of a hypothetical earlier evacuation.

H05. In the aftermath, Kit Ransome prepared a retrospective office report placing the warning before the gate operation. He knew he was reversing the sequence, not correcting an innocent typographical error. Ada approved the account.

H06. Residents continued to contest the official account. Their testimony was not nonexistent or intrinsically unreliable. The recovered roll gives people who privileged the company's records a reason they can no longer readily dismiss; it does not confer reality on an experience that was previously unreal.

H07. A relief fund was established after the flood. Ruth Mercer became its administrator. Ada, local subscribers and other donors contributed. It performed real useful work. Its association with the reassuring public story also helped Ada's reputation. Both things can be true without making every payment fraudulent.

H08. Jonas Reed continued publicly to dispute the warning sequence. He also worked with Ruth to obtain practical support for the affected households. Accepting assistance did not constitute his consent to the official history.

H09. Kit left exchange work and eventually became a comic performer. Clara Harrow later hired him for her small touring theatre company. He told Clara about his part in the retrospective report before the present evening; see the knowledge ledger.

### What the receiver roll does and does not establish

The roll is a continuous paper record from a separate receiving installation in the exchange network. Its relevant function is to record the ordering of identified command/acknowledgement events. It is not an acoustic recording and needs no voice-recognition fiction.

Its event labels, continuity and provenance are subject to examination. When authenticated and read with the other records, it contradicts the approved ordering of warning and gate operation.

It does not contain six tailored revelations, a comprehensive confession or a machine-generated moral verdict. It does not prove that keeping the gate closed would have saved every person, that Ada intended deaths, or that a particular later settlement transfers ownership of an estate.

Separate authorial history from evidence available to a character. H01-H09 are the proposed authored history. A character knows only the facts identified in the knowledge ledger. The roll's contents are narrower than the entire history.

### Why there is an original to recover

The receiving installation was retired after network changes. During cataloguing of its surviving stores for demolition and archive transfer, conservators found the roll among misidentified recording supplies. No suspect secretly maintained a complete, decisive archive for twelve years merely to release it at the right dramatic moment.

Two conservators outside the suspect cast documented the discovery and made a working transcription before the roll was brought to Bellwether House. Their discovery and custody are independently recorded. Their participation occurs before the theft window; neither can become an undeclared seventh culprit.

The transcription survives outside the house. The six participants have received a summary of the relevant reversal before the meeting. Their motives do not depend on learning an unknown revelation after stealing the object.

Possession of the original matters for examining continuity, markings and the working transcription, and for the disputed transfer and public exhibition. Stealing it can delay those processes or redirect custody. It cannot magically make the surviving transcription and witnesses disappear.

## 5. Six people with different lives

These incentives are not six simultaneous theft attempts. In each generated case, only the selected culprit commits the defined taking and concealment. The others can argue, obstruct, negotiate, regret or behave badly without also committing an unmodelled second version of the crime.

### Ada Harrow, approximately 58

Role: owner of Bellwether House and former superintendent of the exchange. She was approximately 46 at the flood.

Ada's governing trait is usefulness. She notices who is cold, which door sticks, who has missed a meal and which practical decision everyone else is avoiding. She is genuinely capable of generosity. She also arranges situations so that generosity leaves her with authority over them.

She is not a helpless widow defending a dead husband's name. She issued the order and approved the false account. She is present, intelligent, still socially effective, and capable of answering back.

Her considered position is that preserving the pumping works was necessary. Her failure is not simply that she wants this claim heard: she treats it as sufficient to settle other people's claims upon her. Being responsible for a necessary act has become, in her mind, almost indistinguishable from being entitled to determine its meaning.

She can admit that the warning was late more easily than she can allow someone else to say what her decision cost. It is her approval of the reordered account that she has particular reason to fear exposing as deliberate conduct.

Life beyond the plot: she enjoys feeding people and amateur practical repairs. She dislikes expensive fittings that do not work. She has a real, argumentative affection for Jonas, who can mend things she would otherwise replace. She remembers members of the relief households individually, not merely as liabilities.

Her daughter Clara remains the person whose disapproval can disrupt Ada's habitual composure. She regularly converts a request for an apology into an offer of help.

Pre-theft expectation: she has read the conservators' summary and knows how it bears on the report she approved. She expects the exhibit to be treated as a verdict on her entire career.

Possible reason to take the roll: delay the handover until her own account is attached to the public presentation. Her incentive is control of interpretation, not the impossible belief that removing the roll erases every witness or copy.

If she is the culprit, she conceals the roll for bargaining and retrieval; she has not already burned it. If she is innocent of tonight's theft, none of her fixed historical responsibility is removed.

Speech: directive, practical, capable of warmth. She answers abstract accusations with decisions, costs and arrangements. Do not make every sentence a polished aphorism or code every kindness as false.

### Jonas Reed, approximately 50

Role: boatbuilder, lower-street resident and organizer among the flood-affected households. He was approximately 38 at the flood and helped with rescue.

Jonas has spent twelve years hearing his account paraphrased by people who were not there. He is particularly angered by praise that turns his rescue work into part of the exchange's reassuring story.

He does not need the roll to discover what happened to his neighborhood. He wants those who discounted its residents to confront evidence produced by the very system they trusted.

Jonas is not a naturally eloquent spokesman for an entire class. He can explain how to get a boat past a submerged wall with extraordinary precision and become impatient when asked to summarize what a community wants. Sometimes he speaks too confidently for people who disagree with him.

He and Ruth Mercer are married. Ruth continues to use her own professional surname; this is not a concealed identity. They have a functioning, affectionate marriage and a consequential disagreement. He thinks accepting Ada's terms perpetuates a humiliating dependency. Ruth thinks refusing available help can impose another cost on people who did not consent to his strategy.

Life beyond the plot: he is patient with children in the workshop, enjoys slow repairs, and remembers who borrowed which tool. He can share an ordinary joke with Ada without forgiving her account of the flood. He does not require purity in everyone he likes.

Pre-theft expectation: the summary corroborates the disputed ordering. He knows the proposed archive transfer could remove the original from easy local access.

Possible reason to take the roll: retain it for a meeting of the lower-street residents before an institution takes custody. He intends the residents to decide what use is made of it. This is unauthorized taking in the present game even if its purpose is sympathetic.

He need not have committed a secret historical wrong to qualify as a suspect. A legitimate claim and an unauthorized means of pursuing it are sufficient.

Speech: specific names, places and physical consequences. He does not speak in dialect spelling. His directness should not mean stupidity or permanent anger.

### Clara Harrow, approximately 31

Role: Ada's daughter and a producer with a small touring theatre company. She was approximately 19 at the flood and worked in the improvised relief arrangements afterward.

Clara has left the house without entirely escaping its economy. Some of the money behind her company came from her mother. Her opposition to Ada is sincere; her material independence is incomplete.

She initially welcomed independent custody of the archive. It offered something she has rarely achieved in an argument with Ada: a process her mother would not chair.

Clara's problem is not merely that she has borrowed money. She wants her mother to recognize that she was wrong. Publicly defeating Ada is not the same as receiving that recognition, and Clara periodically confuses the two.

Kit is an important colleague and a friend. She knows about his part in the false report because he told her before the current dispute, not because she has acquired every secret in the household. The revelation prevents her from dividing the room cleanly into her mother's accomplices and her own people.

Life beyond the plot: she has practical theatre skills, can solve a ruined performance with a few substitutions, and laughs at Kit's least respectable material. She is less efficient about her own difficult conversations. She finds herself issuing Ada-like instructions when a rehearsal goes badly.

Pre-theft expectation: she knows the roll challenges the office report and that Kit may be publicly implicated. She also knows that the current handover permits curatorial selection of what is exhibited.

Possible reason to take the roll: get the complete object into independently documented custody before the agreed transfer can reduce its public presentation to selected excerpts. She wants the evidence preserved and openly examined, but is tempted to decide for everyone else how that openness must happen.

She does not have access to future revelations. Her conflicting loyalties exist before the theft.

Speech: more expansive and self-revising than Ada. Under pressure she begins organizing other people. Her humor comes from performance work, not a constant supply of modern political slogans.

### Kit Ransome, approximately 41

Role: former dispatch clerk, now a comic performer employed at intervals by Clara's company. He was approximately 29 at the flood.

Kit prepared the reordered office report and knew what he was doing. Ada's approval mattered, but he was an adult participant, not an innocent child being used as a writing instrument.

He is neither a concealed mastermind nor a walking confession. He can be very good company. His skill is discovering what a room will let him say if he makes people laugh first. The problem comes when he must say something without that protection.

He has performed imitations of exchange officials, benefactors and his own former pomposity. Ada has laughed at some of them. That ordinary mutual amusement makes their shared wrongdoing more disturbing than a relationship consisting only of threats.

Kit told Clara the essential truth about the false report several months before the discovery of the roll, during a discussion of his former work for material she was commissioning. She did not extract it through a theatrical intuition. His disclosure was a deliberate act with a particular recipient.

Life beyond the plot: he knows how to recover a failed performance, takes inexperienced performers seriously, and is liable to promise help before considering whether he can provide it. Sometimes his cheerfulness is just cheerfulness.

Pre-theft expectation: he knows exactly where his revised report disagrees with the recorded sequence. He knows a transcription already exists. He also knows that his part in the revision will be examined once the material is presented together.

Possible reason to take the roll: postpone the handover long enough to deliver his own signed account of what he did. He wants to stop being introduced only through somebody else's accusation, while attempting one more manipulation of the sequence in which truth arrives.

This motive does not excuse the new theft. Nor must an innocent Kit be secretly trying to destroy the same evidence offstage.

Speech: sociable, rhythmically alert, responsive to the immediate room. At a true admission he becomes plainer rather than more eloquent. Do not turn serious moments into automatic punchlines.

### Ruth Mercer, approximately 47

Role: administrator of the relief fund and Jonas's wife. She was approximately 35 at the flood and helped organize shelter and later support.

Ruth's work is real. It has paid for repairs, tools, accommodation and help that would otherwise not have arrived. She knows that the fund's respectable public presentation made some of that work easier.

She is not secretly embezzling, forging documents or hiding her parentage. The conflict does not need another concealed offense. Her compromise is accepting influence over the form of assistance in order to keep assistance available.

She distrusts the assumption that a truthful public disclosure will automatically produce a just practical result. Her concern is not proof that disclosure must be prevented. She can overestimate how much other people need her to manage a transition.

Her disagreement with Jonas is intimate and specific. They want the residents to have agency; they differ over what actions will actually increase it. Neither has a standing moral veto over the other. Their marriage is not merely the concealed cause of an argument: they still remember one another's habits, exchange jokes and perform small acts of care.

Life beyond the plot: she likes music she can sing with others, is less patient with bad cooking than with bad manners, and can put a hostile visitor at ease. That social ability can be kindness or management, depending on what she does next.

Pre-theft expectation: she has the same summary as the others. She anticipates donors may reconsider the fund when the report is challenged. That is her reasoned fear, not an automatic legal consequence written into the world. The fund is not funded solely by Ada, and no revealed signature magically transfers the estate.

Possible reason to take the roll: delay the next-day presentation while securing independent commitments to the relief households. The risk is that her temporary protective intervention becomes another person's unauthorized postponement of their account.

Speech: names of people, dates, payments and immediate needs; tact without constant evasiveness. She often asks the practical question that neither side of a grand argument has answered.

### Evelyn Marr, approximately 36

Role: collections specialist representing the institution proposing to take the archive. She was approximately 24 at the flood but lived elsewhere and had no part in it.

Evelyn is not secretly a victim's daughter. She is a newcomer with a present stake.

She is professionally good at distinguishing an original from a later transcript, recording custody and preserving fragile material. Her useful expertise must remain real. She should not make elementary mistakes simply to allow the plot to occur.

She has already committed professional time and reputation to the proposed acquisition. Her institution's custody is not yet unconditional; the meeting exists because access and interpretation remain disputed.

Her strongest conviction is that preservation requires someone empowered to say no. In this room, that can become the assumption that those with a personal claim to an object should be kept away from decisions about it. She can describe every fold in a document and still fail to ask who will be able to consult it once she takes it away.

Life beyond the plot: she has an enthusiastic interest in ordinary working objects, enjoys being shown a well-made repair, and is much less formal when she forgets that she is negotiating. Jonas respects that she asks before touching his tools. Their conflict over the roll should not erase this genuine point of contact.

Pre-theft expectation: she knows the summary, the surviving transcription, the planned independent examination and the participants' openly conflicting custody demands. She does not know the historical participants' private intentions by professional intuition.

Possible reason to take the roll: hide it from the disputing parties until she can place it under the custody she considers safer. She calls that protection, but it bypasses the consent the meeting was meant to establish.

Speech: initially precise about procedure; capable of impatient enthusiasm. When pressed about access, she can give an excellent answer to a different technical question. Do not make her use museum jargon in every exchange.

## 6. Relationships that carry scenes

Do not manufacture a different blackmail arrangement for every mathematical pair. Six people permit fifteen pairs, but a pair need not possess a major joint secret to be worth writing.

R01. Ada and Clara: love repeatedly expressed as provision, dissent repeatedly tested through money. Clara wants an acknowledgement that Ada keeps translating into practical assistance. They genuinely enjoy some of the same company.

R02. Jonas and Ruth: an affectionate marriage with a sustained argument about agency, assistance and who bears the cost of moral gestures. This is not a manufactured marital betrayal or a secret they hide from the room.

R03. Ada and Kit: two participants in the false account whose relationship also includes mundane shared work and real amusement. Both know what they did and know that the other knows. No unnecessary second-order concealment is required.

R04. Clara and Kit: artistic trust complicated by a disclosure made before the present plot. Clara cannot demand a simple purge of her mother's collaborators without confronting her own affection for one of them.

R05. Ada and Ruth: a productive alliance with a distribution of power neither can pretend is equal. Ada has helped the fund; Ruth has helped make Ada's benefaction effective and respectable. Neither fact decides every later obligation.

R06. Jonas and Evelyn: respect for craft colliding with disagreement about custody. She understands the value of preserving the thing; he insists upon preserving the affected people's access and authority as well.

Secondary relationships can develop out of these without adding secrets. Jonas and Clara may agree about Ada and disagree about what independence costs. Kit and Ruth can enjoy an ordinary song. Evelyn and Clara can favor independent preservation while differing over which institution should control it. Leave room for scenes to discover distinctions rather than assigning fifteen ready-made revelations.

K02. Deliberate alteration of the retrospective office report.
Direct participants: Ada and Kit.
Additional direct disclosure: Kit told Clara several months before the roll was discovered.
Others: can compare the reported sequence with the new summary and reasonably suspect alteration. They do not automatically know the content of the private post-flood conversation.

K03. Jonas's disagreement with the official account.
Source: his public statements and the known complaints of the lower-street residents.
Known by: the other participants through those statements; not a concealed family relationship.

K04. The relief fund's financial arrangements.
Source: fund accounts and renewal discussions.

## 8. Present-case contract

The new second category is proposed as a concealment object, not a vague technique. This changes the fiction of the existing method slot; it does not add a category or automatically alter engine code.

The player identifies:
1. Who removed the original receiver roll.
2. Which designated portable object contains it.
3. In which room that object is concealed at the investigation's start.

For this proposed case family, the roll remains intact, inside one selected object, in one selected room throughout the investigation. It is not carried on a suspect, moved during play, sent outside, burned, divided or nested in two candidate objects. An intention to remove or destroy it later is not an accomplished act.

Only one suspect performs the taking and concealment. Innocent suspects can be morally compromised without becoming undeclared accomplices or additional thieves.

The cabinet-access step is fixed, not another concealed answer: the ordinary cabinet key is used and returned. All six know where it is kept and have the ordinary access needed to obtain it. No suspect requires a unique technical skill. The case should not require a lockpick as one unannounced method in addition to the selected object.

### Opening and custody schedule

T01. Before the gathering: external conservators document recovery, make the working transcription, and transfer the sealed protective packet containing the original to the scheduled examination. The packet identification and recorded contents are checked by two independent examiners. Existing copies remain outside the house.

T02. Immediately before the private consultation: those examiners verify the original and its protective packet, place it in the record-wing cabinet, and lock it. Verification must concern the original contents, not merely an envelope presumed to contain them.

T03. The six participants spend a thirty-minute private consultation interval in the nine-room record wing. The cabinet key is in its familiar labelled key drawer, available to the invited custodians. The ordinary house practice trusts this invited group rather than treating the cabinet as a high-security vault. Evelyn has recommended tighter custody; Ada has insisted the private discussion proceed on the familiar arrangement. This disagreement is explicit and does not make Evelyn professionally unaware of the risk.

T04. Entry points to the record wing are monitored by stewards. The household staff, outside conservators and investigators do not enter during this interval. Windows and other external access points are accounted for. This is a bounded case fact, not an inference based on somebody's social class. No unobserved servant, messenger, outside climber or accomplice is an eligible hidden actor.

T05. There is sufficient unobserved movement inside for any of the six to reach the cabinet, use the key, select an accessible portable object, conceal the packet in it, place it in any of the nine rooms and return. No fixed dialogue, compulsory seat assignment or decorative timetable may accidentally give one suspect a complete alibi. Initial investigator positions later generated by the engine do not describe the suspects' positions during this earlier interval.

T06. At the end of the interval, the six assemble for the reading and do not re-enter the record wing. The examiners discover the original missing. The returned key and cabinet condition do not identify a thief or a concealment object. The cabinet opening is no longer a separately randomized mystery.

T07. Investigation begins. Suspects remain together outside the examined rooms; the original's position cannot change. A full case reconstruction after solution identifies taking, object and hiding place. No opening announcement, artwork or fixed item placement may already settle either hidden category.

The exact staged movements and evidence for perimeter closure still require a production script. The above is the proposed common contract, not a claim that 324 detailed scenes have been implemented or physically playtested.

### Six concealment objects

Proposed player labels:
1. Dispatch bag.
2. Tea caddy.
3. Sewing box.
4. Plate case.
5. Document tube.
6. Tool chest.

These are designated portable house objects involved in preparing the collection, not six items permanently assigned to six suspects. A plate case is a carrying case for photographic plates; use that fuller description in the glossary. Specify a small portable tool chest, not a fixed workshop fixture.

The original is a small rolled paper strip in a protective packet sized to fit every candidate. Each object's proposed design must provide the required space without specialist disassembly, damage, a flame, another candidate object or impossible handling. Interior dimensions need to be fixed when assets are specified.

Opening or observing any room does not automatically perform a complete physical search. Room entry enables the game's structured investigation. Do not promise a free-form room-search simulator while retaining an abstract card mechanic.

The six objects must be visually and verbally distinguishable, but no puzzle depends upon recognizing their pictures. A tea caddy can conceal documentary evidence as readily as a technical case; class-coded ownership must not constrain the generated answer.

Nonselected object positions are not established by this document. They must not be fixed by atmosphere or art in ways that contradict the selected case. The fact that a candidate exists does not announce whether it is currently in the room being described.

## 10. Evidence and interaction boundaries

The engine's categories remain independent unless a later explicit rules change says otherwise. Six suspects, six objects and nine rooms produce 324 potential triples. A category-label change does not itself validate those triples.

An initial plausibility check removes the former dependence on specialist sabotage: all suspects can carry the protective packet and all six portable objects; all rooms are reachable; no object belongs exclusively to a suspect; the original remains in a static final location. Detailed timings, object dimensions and case-conditioned evidence still need validation. Do not claim exhaustive validation from the fact that labels can be multiplied.

Three investigators can still receive six cards apiece from the eighteen non-solution cards. The arithmetic does not prove three is the best number of players.

A held suspect card excludes that person from the present taking. It does not establish moral innocence concerning the flood. A held object card excludes that concealment object; a held room card excludes that final location.

Historical dialogue and documents do not automatically become case evidence. The public history can remain the same while the selected thief changes. A fixed line about someone's character is not an alibi. A case-specific claim requires a traceable permitted fact.

The investigator opponents have different permitted views of the present case. They are not the suspects speaking misleadingly through refutation. Do not replace compulsory truthful card responses with untracked lies by the character cast.

Retain the existing investigator names as engine/tutorial labels for now. Their full-world casting and histories are not silently settled by this proposal. Professionally, a light frame is that they are independent examiners invited for the archive transfer, whose assignment becomes reconstruction of the theft. Private hands, turn order, competitive answers and the failed-accusation rule remain acknowledged deduction-game conventions. Do not invent a historical law that requires professional investigators to withhold exculpatory facts merely to justify the board game.

The manually controlled turn structure has no real-time deadline. A handover planned for the next morning is narrative pressure, not a hidden timer or a reason to rush a screen-reader user.

## 11. A tone sample, not a fixed case event

This scene illustrates relationships before any case-specific accusation. It is not a required transcript and contains no generated alibi or hidden-answer clue.

Ada sets a dish beside Jonas.

"You haven't eaten."

"I have."

Ruth says, "He hasn't. Thank you."

Jonas accepts the plate. Ada sits down.

Evelyn opens the proposed agreement. "There are still the conditions of access to settle."

"They can be settled here," Jonas says.

"They can. The record cannot remain indefinitely in an unsuitable cabinet."

He asks, "Will Ruth be able to read it without asking permission three weeks beforehand?"

Evelyn turns back a page.

"We can write that in," Clara says.

"And Mrs. Dean?" Jonas asks. "She doesn't write letters."

Ada says, "Then someone can write one for her."

Ruth puts down her fork. "That is the part he is objecting to, Ada."

A supporting name in a tone sample does not add a suspect or establish a hidden family connection. The sample's point is that an access dispute can arise through ordinary social interaction without a round of six concealed threats.

## 13. Governing dramatic distinction

Ada wants to attach her account.
Jonas wants the affected households to hold the original.
Clara wants independent public examination and is not immune to imposing her own terms.
Kit wants to choose when he admits his part.
Ruth wants continuity of practical support while the record is disclosed.
Evelyn wants custody she considers competent and safe.

They are not six versions of the same villain. They are six people with different claims upon a thing none of them should be allowed to redefine merely by possessing it.

Solving the case identifies who took the roll. It does not automatically settle whose claim on its future is just.
